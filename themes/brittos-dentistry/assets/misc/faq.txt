Dental Treatment FAQ Sheet
Helpful Questions Every Patient Should Know Before Treatment
1. Dental Implants
Q1. What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is a titanium post surgically placed into the jawbone to replace a missing tooth root. It supports a crown, bridge, or denture.

Q2. Are dental implants painful?

Most patients report minimal discomfort. The procedure is usually performed under local anesthesia, and any post-operative soreness is typically manageable with medication.

Q3. How long do dental implants last?

With proper oral hygiene and regular dental check-ups, dental implants can last 15 to 25 years or even a lifetime.

Q4. Am I a suitable candidate for dental implants?

Most adults with healthy gums and adequate jawbone density are suitable candidates. A consultation and imaging scan will determine eligibility.

Q5. How long does the implant process take?

The entire process generally takes 3 to 6 months, depending on healing time and whether bone grafting is needed.

2. Root Canal Treatment (RCT)
Q1. Why would I need a root canal?

A root canal is needed when the tooth pulp becomes infected due to deep decay, fractures, or trauma.

Q2. Is a root canal treatment painful?

Modern root canal treatments are generally no more uncomfortable than getting a filling because local anesthesia is used.

Q3. How many visits are required?

Many root canals can be completed in one or two appointments, depending on the complexity of the case.

Q4. Will my tooth need a crown afterward?

In most cases, especially for back teeth, a crown is recommended to protect the treated tooth from fracture.

Q5. What happens if I delay treatment?

Delaying treatment can lead to worsening infection, severe pain, abscess formation, and possible tooth loss.

3. Teeth Whitening
Q1. How does professional teeth whitening work?

Professional whitening uses safe bleaching agents to remove stains and brighten the color of your teeth.

Q2. Is teeth whitening safe?

Yes. When performed by a qualified dental professional, teeth whitening is considered safe and effective.

Q3. How long do whitening results last?

Results can last from 6 months to 3 years depending on oral hygiene, diet, and lifestyle habits.

Q4. Will whitening make my teeth sensitive?

Some temporary sensitivity may occur, but it usually subsides within a few days.

Q5. Does whitening work on crowns and fillings?

No. Whitening only affects natural tooth enamel and does not change the color of restorations.

4. Orthodontic Braces
Q1. At what age can someone get braces?

Braces can benefit both children and adults. Treatment may begin as early as age 7 if required.

Q2. How long will I need to wear braces?

Treatment duration typically ranges from 12 to 30 months depending on the complexity of the case.

Q3. Do braces hurt?

You may feel mild pressure or soreness after adjustments, but this usually resolves within a few days.

Q4. What foods should I avoid?

Sticky, hard, and chewy foods should be avoided to prevent damage to brackets and wires.

Q5. Will I need to wear retainers afterward?

Yes. Retainers help maintain the results achieved through orthodontic treatment.

5. Clear Aligners
Q1. What are clear aligners?

Clear aligners are transparent, removable trays designed to gradually move teeth into proper alignment.

Q2. How many hours should aligners be worn daily?

For best results, aligners should be worn for 20 to 22 hours per day.

Q3. Are aligners better than braces?

Both are effective. Aligners are often preferred for aesthetics and convenience, while braces may be advantageous for complex cases.

Q4. Can I eat while wearing aligners?

No. Aligners should be removed before eating or drinking anything other than water.

Q5. How often are aligners changed?

Most patients switch to a new set of aligners every one to two weeks as directed by the dentist.

6. Tooth Extraction
Q1. Why might a tooth need to be extracted?

Extraction may be necessary due to severe decay, advanced gum disease, infection, trauma, or overcrowding.

Q2. Is the procedure painful?

The extraction itself is typically painless due to anesthesia. Some discomfort afterward is normal.

Q3. How long does healing take?

Initial healing usually occurs within 1 to 2 weeks, while complete bone healing may take several months.

Q4. What is a dry socket?

Dry socket occurs when the protective blood clot is lost prematurely, exposing bone and causing pain.

Q5. What should I avoid after extraction?

Avoid smoking, drinking through straws, vigorous rinsing, and strenuous activities for a few days.

7. Wisdom Tooth Removal
Q1. Why are wisdom teeth often removed?

They may become impacted, cause pain, damage neighboring teeth, or increase the risk of infection.

Q2. Does everyone need wisdom teeth removed?

No. Removal is only recommended when they pose current or future dental problems.

Q3. How long is the recovery period?

Most patients recover comfortably within 3 to 7 days.

Q4. Can wisdom teeth crowd other teeth?

Impacted wisdom teeth may contribute to pressure on surrounding teeth in some cases.

Q5. What foods can I eat after removal?

Soft foods such as yogurt, soups, mashed potatoes, and smoothies are generally recommended initially.

8. Dental Crowns
Q1. What is a dental crown?

A crown is a custom-made cap placed over a damaged or weakened tooth to restore strength and appearance.

Q2. When is a crown recommended?

Crowns are commonly used after root canal treatment, large fillings, fractures, or severe wear.

Q3. How long do crowns last?

Most crowns last 10 to 15 years or longer with proper care.

Q4. Can crowns look natural?

Yes. Modern ceramic and zirconia crowns are designed to closely match natural teeth.

Q5. Do crowned teeth require special care?

No special care is required beyond good oral hygiene and regular dental visits.

9. Dental Veneers
Q1. What are veneers?

Veneers are thin shells placed on the front surface of teeth to improve appearance.

Q2. What cosmetic issues can veneers correct?

They can improve discoloration, chips, gaps, uneven teeth, and minor alignment concerns.

Q3. Are veneers permanent?

Traditional veneers usually require removal of a small amount of enamel, making them a long-term treatment.

Q4. How long do veneers last?

Depending on the material and maintenance, veneers can last 10 to 15 years or longer.

Q5. Will veneers stain?

Porcelain veneers are highly resistant to stains compared to natural teeth.

10. Gum Disease Treatment
Q1. What are the early signs of gum disease?

Common signs include bleeding gums, redness, swelling, and persistent bad breath.

Q2. Can gum disease be reversed?

Early-stage gum disease (gingivitis) can often be reversed with proper treatment and improved oral hygiene.

Q3. What is scaling and root planing?

It is a deep-cleaning procedure that removes plaque and tartar below the gumline.

Q4. Is gum disease linked to overall health?

Research suggests gum health may be connected to overall systemic health and inflammation.

Q5. How can I prevent gum disease?

Brush twice daily, floss regularly, maintain professional cleanings, and avoid tobacco use.

11. Pediatric Dentistry
Q1. When should my child first visit the dentist?

Children should typically visit a dentist by their first birthday or within six months of the first tooth erupting.

Q2. Why are baby teeth important?

Baby teeth assist with chewing, speech development, and proper spacing for permanent teeth.

Q3. How often should children have dental check-ups?

Most children benefit from dental visits every six months.

Q4. What causes cavities in children?

Frequent sugary snacks, poor brushing habits, and prolonged bottle feeding can increase cavity risk.

Q5. Are dental X-rays safe for children?

Dental X-rays use very low radiation levels and are considered safe when clinically necessary.

12. Professional Dental Cleaning
Q1. Why do I need professional cleaning if I brush daily?

Professional cleanings remove hardened plaque (tartar) that cannot be removed by brushing alone.

Q2. How often should I have a dental cleaning?

Most patients benefit from a cleaning every six months, though some may require more frequent visits.

Q3. Does cleaning damage tooth enamel?

No. Professional cleaning safely removes deposits without harming healthy enamel.

Q4. Why do my gums bleed during cleaning?

Bleeding may indicate inflammation or gum disease that requires attention.

Q5. Can cleaning help prevent bad breath?

Yes. Removing plaque, bacteria, and tartar can significantly improve oral freshness.
